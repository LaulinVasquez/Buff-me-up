## Implemented experience

Five request-level styles: Balanced, Vegan, Vegetarian, Pescatarian and Meat lovers. Users choose new ideas plus favorites, recipe ideas, or saved meals only; and More variety or Meal prep. These choices are restored with the draft. They do not change permanent diet preferences. Vegan/vegetarian/pescatarian styles add filters, and incompatible animal-food styles are disabled/rejected for restricted profiles. All sources retain profile exclusions, declared allergens, active-time/budget estimates and reviewed status for saved meals.

The app-authored catalog contains **43 recipes / 37 ingredient definitions**, including breakfast, lunch, dinner and snacks. A complete profile can generate a week with an empty saved library. Missing preferences go to the questions, then return directly to generation; saved-only users retain guided meal setup. Different choices are selected before reuse, with adjacent repeats avoided when alternatives exist. Meal prep intentionally pairs days 1/2, 3/4 and 5/6 while changing between batches; day 7 uses another least-used choice. Scarcity is visible rather than hidden by copying the same day. Recent habits and usual-food notes have a small tie-breaking role, alongside food-group/goal preferences.

New recipe previews include gram quantities, two-serving yield, declared allergens/patterns, estimated active time/cost and preparation steps. Users review each selected recipe once, or adapt it in the existing recipe/product editor. Adaptation stays in the plan draft; it does not save a meal early. The editor keeps quantities/position through reload, preserves the existing inline cooking-limit update, and forces review of changed inputs. The final week review covers choices/servings, and saving atomically adds selected reviewed recipes together with the plan. Identical active reviewed recipes are reused across weeks without overwriting edits. Recipes and preparation directions persist in immutable plan snapshots; saved guides stay collapsed until opened. Planning never creates food logs.

The preparation guide includes ingredient portions, cooked-grain preparation, a midweek refresh, storage/freezing and temperature references. Optional AI chooses supported preparation priorities and known compatible meal aliases; the app supplies recipe directions. It does **not** produce unrestricted novel cooking directions or nutrient values. Style, source, mode, reviews, edits and retry tokens survive reload; restored AI remains unchecked. Preference revisions invalidate new-recipe reviews. An in-flight virtual recipe remains resolvable after its saved counterpart arrives, including a committed-but-lost-response retry.

## Nutrition and personalization

The planner uses saved diet/exclusions, time, budget, food habits and qualitative goal/food-group preferences. Measurements stay private and are not used to invent calorie needs. An optional, explicitly selected existing user calorie target can guide bounded half-serving choices only on days with complete calorie estimates; incomplete days retain their servings. Targets are never changed and no energy deficit is prescribed. Portions remain manually editable.

With the existing server USDA key, public catalog ingredients are matched against Foundation/SR Legacy search records. The matcher requires the named cooked/raw/drained, skinless or low-fat forms where applicable, rejects branded/wrong-form results, retains FDC IDs/retrieval times and computes totals with the shared calculator. These are generic estimates, not a guarantee of the user's exact package. Users can replace ingredients with their actual products using food search or manual labels. Unknown nutrients remain null; zero-known-calorie previews say “Nutrition pending.” Without the key, on quota/provider failure, or for uncertain matches, recipes still work with visibly unknown nutrition.

Successful public ingredient matches are cached for 24 hours; missing/failed matches use a five-minute process-local backoff rather than a day-long negative cache, with at most six concurrent lookups and an 8.5-second preview wait; individual requests time out at seven seconds. At most the catalog's 37 fixed ingredient definitions are considered. No private habit notes/profile/raw diary enters USDA searches. A food-request quota check precedes enrichment. Late in-flight public cache lookups may finish after the preview returns; no more jobs are scheduled after its deadline. No extra provider, dependency, image license or environment variable was added.

OpenAI remains separately optional with the existing server key/model, current scoped consent, explicit checkbox, latest-consent recheck, ten-attempt database quota, 15-second timeout, 3,000-output-token bound and `store:false`. Shared data stays limited to meal aliases/names/types/tags/counts, broad preferences and the selected style/mode. Measurements, targets, exclusions, package lists and raw diary are excluded. Unknown aliases, invalid/refused/incomplete output, unsupported prep priorities, poorer variety or broken prep pairs are rejected; fallback is visibly labeled. No live provider call or charge was made in development. Official response contract: [OpenAI structured outputs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs?api-mode=responses).

Food sources: [USDA FoodData Central API and attribution](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/api-guide/). General preparation/storage references: [USDA leftovers](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety), [FoodSafety.gov temperatures](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures). App recipes/variety scores are not a USDA certification, complete nutrient-adequacy assessment or medical diet. Existing qualitative guidance version remains unchanged.
